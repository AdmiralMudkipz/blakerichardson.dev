Why I'm Running

Our township desperately needs transparency.

Meeting minutes get posted, but minutes are not the same as being in the room. During COVID we could join township meetings from anywhere, and that access is gone. I will bring back live online access and publish recordings of every meeting, so busy working people can watch, listen, and have a voice in our future as well.

Our township can do far more with state grants.

These grants can be used for paths, crosswalks, and safety improvements around schools and playgrounds. I will push to take advantage of all these grants to improve our town. Using state money you've already paid for, instead of costing us on the local level.

We must make it easier for everyone to build solar.

Our township has backwards, decade old solar laws that make building solar a time consuming headache. Solar is now the most affordable source of electricity, in a world where AI is spiking our utility costs, we deserve to have Power over our own Power.

If we tried to build Main Street today, it would be illegal.

New development lately has been completely different from what made our Harrison great. I want our town to grow with the same charm and character that founded it, and we can start by relegalizing traditional forms of development that made our town what it is today.

Maximize the Grants

State, federal, and regional programs that can pay for safer streets and a more connected town, with no new local tax burden.

Connect our schools, neighborhoods, and Main Street

  • Use bike lane and path grants to link neighborhoods to our schools, so more kids can get there safely without a car. That means less morning drop-off and pickup traffic and less strain on the bus system.
  • Use Complete Streets planning in high foot traffic areas and places with a known speeding problem, with raised crosswalks and curb extensions near crosswalks to slow drivers down.
  • People adapt their speed to the road. When roads are designed like racetracks, with wide lanes and large shoulders, people speed. Narrowing lanes & shoulders slightly in problem areas is proven to dramatically cut speeding.
  • Pedestrian death rates climb sharply between 30 and 40 mph, so getting drivers to slow down even a little in high foot traffic areas can be the difference between life and death.
  • Walkable areas work as a network. The more people who can safely walk to Main Street, the more foot traffic and customers our Main Street businesses get.
