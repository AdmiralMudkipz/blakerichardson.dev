Issue Brief

Cut Red Tape

Less bureaucracy, clearer local rules, and faster outcomes for residents and small businesses.

Issue & Policy: Transparency / Livestream Township Meetings

Livestream Township Meetings

Township meetings are livestreamed on YouTube where videos will automatically be archived for future reference.

Effects: Community members will have easy and open access to the township meetings and information.

Issue & Policy: Remove costly setback mandates

Decrease Setbacks

Require adequate setbacks for on-street parking, space for walking/biking, but nothing excessive. Parking lots can be placed behind buildings.

Effects: Relegalizes traditional Main Street style storefronts, limits land waste while ensuring that adequate walking, cycling and on-street parking space is still preserved where necessary.

Issue & Policy: Parking Reform / Reforming Costly Parking Mandates

Reform Costly Parking Mandates

Current parking mandates often require more spaces than are ever actually used, leaving giant ugly empty lots, and business owners' money, wasting the town's land and squandering potential tax revenue from better land use. The amount of parking should be decided by actual demand, the property owner decides how much parking is necessary for their property. These laws were made 48 years ago and with no real basis on current needs or usage patterns.

Effects: Allows more flexible land use around businesses. Creates job opportunities. Increases potential commercial tax base. Stops development from destroying our farmland and forests.

Issue & Policy: Tax-Base Development / More Main Street

More Main Street

Properties within a mile of a Main Street building can be transformed into a Main Street style mixed-use building, consumer commercial first floor, residential/office space on above floors, with Main Street frontage, provided they follow the aesthetic of Main Street buildings.

Effects: Relegalizes Main Street style development on properties bordering and surrounding Main Street. Raises more revenue for the town without raising your taxes.

Issue & Policy: Housing / Legalize In-Law Suites

Legalize In-Law Suites

Allow accessory dwelling units on existing residential plots provided they are 1,000 sq. ft; or 50% of the footprint of the primary residence, whichever is smaller.

Effects: Allows affordable housing construction on existing properties without requiring additional utility and road costs. Lowers cost of living. Helps get down our affordable housing quota without bulldozing farmland and forest. Less traffic than sprawl development. Raises more revenue for the town without raising your taxes.

Issue & Policy: Enable Small Business / Café & Bakery Law

Café & Bakery Law

Businesses can operate from residential properties near Main Street without rezoning, provided:

  • They pass all necessary inspections and certifications like they would in a commercial building.
  • At least one floor of said building is still dedicated to a housing unit.
  • Possible businesses could include but not be limited to: cafés, bakeries, grocery stores, delis, restaurants, stores, offices, and gathering places.
  • Businesses must also not emit more air pollution than the average house, and must follow the same noise standards as a normal residence, with mandatory soundproofing of the building.
  • The aesthetic style of the building fits the rest of the neighborhood, and/or follows the style of Main Street.

Effects: Relegalizes small businesses to start up and operate within our communities. Gives opportunities for community members to start businesses out of their homes. Creates job and enterprise opportunities. Reduces traffic by giving residents amenities within walking distance. Fosters local community spirit by creating meeting places and community gathering spaces within preexisting communities.

Issue & Policy: Housing / Lot Size Reform

Lot Size Reform

Remove the minimum lot size for all sewer-connected properties. If the sewer capacity is at its limit a builder should be allowed to build given they contribute funds for the necessary capacity upgrades to the water treatment system.

Effects: Ensures sewer and water infrastructure expands with the demand needed by the town without excessive expense to existing residences. Stops development from destroying our farmland and forests.

Issue & Policy: Sustainable Development Planning / Commercial Subsidized Affordable Housing

Commercial Subsidized Affordable Housing

Our town was mixed-use from the very beginning, some of the oldest Main Street buildings are apartments on top of businesses. Affordable housing is state-mandated anyway, our town has to build it no matter what, but we can ensure it matches our town character. This policy focuses on integrating commercial mixed-use into those developments. A corner store or bakery at the end of a block of apartments, for example, creates a walkable community instead of sprawl. The goal is to keep affordable housing tax-positive for the town while minimizing traffic by placing homes and everyday services together.

Effects: Meets state housing requirements, reduces traffic through having local services, leaves existing neighborhoods untouched, and helps ensure affordable housing contributes net-positive tax revenue to support schools and roads.
